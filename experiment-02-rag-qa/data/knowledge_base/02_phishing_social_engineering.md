# Phishing and Social Engineering

## What is Phishing?
Phishing is a fraudulent social engineering technique where attackers impersonate trustworthy entities (such as banks, IT administrators, or executive leadership) via email, messaging, or phone to trick individuals into disclosing sensitive information, such as login credentials, credit card details, or personal data.

## Common Types of Phishing Attacks
1. **Spear Phishing:** Highly targeted phishing campaigns directed at specific individuals or organizations, customized using researched personal details.
2. **Whaling:** A specialized form of spear phishing aimed specifically at high-profile executives, such as CEOs, CFOs, or senior managers, to authorize fraudulent financial transfers.
3. **Smishing and Vishing:** Phishing conducted via SMS text messages (Smishing) or voice phone calls (Vishing).
4. **Baiting:** Promising a reward (e.g., free software or infected USB drives left in public areas) to lure victims into compromising security.

## Indicators of Phishing Emails
- Generic greetings instead of personalized names.
- Mismatched domain names in sender email addresses.
- Urgent or threatening language demanding immediate action.
- Suspicious hyperlinked URLs pointing to fraudulent lookalike web domains.
- Unexpected email attachments containing executable scripts or macro-enabled documents.

## Mitigation and Defense Strategies
- Deploy domain authentication protocols like SPF, DKIM, and DMARC to block spoofed email senders.
- Require multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all corporate login portals.
- Conduct regular employee security awareness training and simulated phishing testing.
